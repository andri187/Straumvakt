# Scope and sprints — re-evaluated 2026-08-04

**Supersedes the sequencing in** [`GOING_PUBLIC_CRITICAL_PATH.md`](./GOING_PUBLIC_CRITICAL_PATH.md)
(ratified 2026-06-04). That document's *order* was right and remains the
spine; its *starting point* was wrong — P1 was never startable, because

## What is actually true, 2026-08-04

**Built and working:** the OCPP engine (Durable Objects, hibernation,
outbound commands); the asset chain property → site → installation →
circuit → charger → EVSE → connector; sessions, OCMF evidence, protocol
log, retention and partitions; identity with composable organisation
roles; the Zaptec vendor path; a large operator console; a driver app at
roughly 70%; 14 driver endpoints live.

**Decided but unbuilt:** billing (three model generations, all empty),
load balancing, DSO comms, the issues engine.

**Broken or missing:** `createDriver` unwired, so four of five paths
issue no charging token; RLS not enforced; MFA off; production is a
37 MB branch from April that predates everything.

**The discovery problem.** In one session on 2026-08-03/04, nine things
were found already built that nobody knew about: `check:schema-parity`,
`idtag-classifier.ts`, `entitlements.FeatureFlag`, `OrganizationRole`
(13 values, composable), `iceland-energy-parties.json`, the DSOF / REPF /
CHRGRF cost factors, `VendorAssetRef.credentialsRef`,
`GOING_PUBLIC_CRITICAL_PATH` itself, and ADR 0025. Three ADRs were
written duplicating ground already covered by ten others.

**45 ADRs exceed what one person can hold**, so each new question gets
answered fresh instead of looked up. That is how three billing
generations happened — nobody decided to model billing three times.
**This is the largest risk in the project, and it is cheap to fix once.**

## S0 · Stop the invisible failures

**Ahead of everything. Small, and the reason it is first is below.**

- `ocpp_identities.last_seen_at` must come from **OCPP frames**. It
  currently reflects the Zaptec poll, so every charger read "online for
  6d 15h" throughout a three-month total OCPP outage. Keep the vendor
  timestamp as a separate field; one column cannot honestly mean both.
- **Stable custom hostname** for the gateway. Chargers currently hold
  `straumvakt-ocpp-staging.straumvakt.workers.dev`, which is tied to a
  worker name and an environment. Rename either and all 21 go dark
  again, silently, exactly as they did in May. Per-tenant hostnames are
  the natural shape.
- **Connection alerting** — a charger that stops speaking should raise
  something within the hour, not within a quarter.

> On 13 May 2026 at ~09:00 UTC, thirteen Dalvegur chargers disconnected
> within fifty seconds of each other and did not return until a portal
> re-point on 4 August. Nothing anywhere reported it. It was found by
> reading a warning in Zaptec's own portal. No code was wrong — the
> gateway worked throughout. **An outage detector matters more than any
> feature when the outage lasted a quarter of a year.**

## Decision 2026-08-04 — any party can be Straumvakt's customer

**Operator decision. Affects S1 and S2 directly.**

Straumvakt remains an **agent** for the electricity transaction — ADR 0031
§1 stands, the host is still principal, nothing about the energy chain
changes. What is removed is a different boundary: the implicit assumption
that **Straumvakt's own customer must be a host organisation.**

Any identity on the platform can hold an agreement with Straumvakt for the
platform service:

| counterparty | case |
|---|---|
| organisation (host) | húsfélag, employer, forecourt operator |
| organisation (non-host) | a contractor paying for their fleet view |
| **organisation (retailer)** | an energy company reselling charging |
| **individual** | a homeowner with one charger; a driver on a paid tier |

### Structural consequence

**An agreement's counterparty must be a party, not an organisation.**
`agreements.counterparty_org_id` encodes the boundary being removed. It
needs to resolve to either an organisation or a person.

The alternative — making every individual an Organization — is how systems
end up with tens of thousands of one-person "companies". Not that.

**This must be settled in S1, before a billing generation is chosen.**
Whichever generation survives will hard-code its counterparty assumption,
and an org-only one would need migrating again within the year.

### It also resolves the homeless driver

ADR 0043 allows a driver with no organisation. Under the old assumption
such a driver was unbillable, so "homeless" read as a waiting state. Under
this decision they are billable directly by Straumvakt, and homeless
becomes a legitimate permanent condition rather than an incomplete one.

### Why it matters commercially

The addressable market stops being "charge point operators in Iceland" — a
handful of companies — and becomes **every electricity retailer who needs
an EV offering.**

`docs/reference/iceland-energy-parties.json` already shows they are not
hypothetical. Seven retailers, all with published tariff tables, several
already carrying the `home_charging` role:

- **ON** — 20 tariff items including Heimahleðsla at 2 900 / 4 400 /
  1 190 kr per month, plus workplace tiers
- **Orkusalan** 14 · **HS Veitur** 19 · **Straumlind** 8 · **HS Orka** 6 ·
  **N1 Rafmagn** 5 · **Orka heimilanna** 4

They are already selling EV charging and competing on it. None of them
wants to build a CPMS. In a competitive retail market, charging is how a
retailer defends its book — which makes the platform infrastructure they
buy rather than a threat to their customer relationship, consistent with
the agent posture in ADR 0031.

And it compounds: a retailer as customer brings their charging customers,
whose own retail supplier is then visible — the cross-sell signal pointing
back at itself.

### Follow-on

`home_charging` still has no `OrganizationRole` value, so the retailers
whose only role is charger rental (Fallorka, Amper) currently sit with
empty role arrays. Adding it — along with `producer` and `aggregator` —
is S1 work and is now commercially load-bearing rather than tidiness.
